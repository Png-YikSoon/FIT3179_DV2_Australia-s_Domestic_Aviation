Up in the Air: Exploring Australia’s Domestic Aviation

Australia’s vast landscape and coastal population centres make domestic aviation an important link between cities and regional destinations. This visualisation examines passenger trends, popular routes and seasonal demand, while exploring how Australians travel by air for work and leisure.

Section 1

The Big Picture: Australia’s Domestic Aviation Trends and Recovery

Australia’s Domestic Airline Passenger Trends, 2014–2026

Passengers on domestic flights each month (thin line) and the
12-month average (thick line). Hover for exact figures.

Key findings

97% collapse during COVID-19

Monthly passenger numbers plunged to just 146,000 in April 2020, 97% lower than in April 2019.

Uneven recovery in 2020–2021

Border restrictions and the Delta lockdowns caused repeated disruptions to domestic passenger demand.

Domestic Air Travel Returns to Pre-Pandemic Levels

Domestic passenger numbers recovered strongly by 2023 and returned to broadly pre-pandemic levels by 2024–2026, demonstrating a sustained recovery following COVID-19 disruptions.

Key findings

Metropolitan hubs dominate passenger traffic

Melbourne and Sydney handle substantial passenger volumes, while Brisbane and Perth serve as major domestic aviation hubs. Their large populations and concentration of business activity generate strong travel demand.

East coast connectivity

Passenger traffic is concentrated around Melbourne, Sydney and Brisbane, reflecting their large populations, economic activity and strong connections between major cities.

Lower traffic at regional airports

Regional airports generally handle fewer passengers than metropolitan hubs due to smaller populations and fewer flight connections. However, tourism and industry can generate substantial traffic at some regional airports.

Changes in Australia’s Busiest Domestic Flight Routes, 2019–2025

Percentage change in passenger numbers across the 20 busiest domestic
routes in 2019, comparing 2025 with the pre-pandemic baseline.

Key findings

Brisbane–Perth leads growth

Passenger numbers rose by 19% from 2019 to 2025, the largest increase among the 20 busiest routes of 2019. This highlights growth on selected routes, although the reasons remain unclear.

Route recovery remains uneven

Of the 20 routes, 10 remained below 2019 passenger levels, 8 exceeded them and 2 were essentially unchanged, showing that recovery varied considerably by route.

Canberra routes record the largest declines

Canberra–Sydney fell by 34%, while Canberra–Melbourne declined by 17%. These substantial drops warrant further investigation into changes in demand, flight frequency or airline capacity.

Changes in Australia’s Domestic Aviation Activity Between 2019 and 2025

Percentage change in six national measures for all domestic airlines,
from 2019 to 2025. Each line starts at 0%, its
2019 level. Rising lines are higher than before the pandemic;
falling lines are lower.

Key findings

Fewer Flights, Higher Passenger Loads

Flights fell by 5.5%, while passengers per flight rose by 3.9%. This suggests airlines carried more passengers on fewer flights, indicating greater passenger concentration per service.

Passenger Demand Remains Below 2019

Total passenger numbers remained 1.8% below 2019 levels, despite seat occupancy increasing by 2.2%. Higher occupancy therefore did not translate into a full recovery in overall passenger volume.

Key findings

Brisbane Is the Best-Connected Hub

Brisbane features in 19 routes, followed by Sydney (18) and Melbourne (15), making them the network’s most connected cities.

Perth Connects Distant Destinations

Perth has 11 routes, including connections to Karratha and Port Hedland. Brisbane–Perth is the longest route at approximately 3,615 km, highlighting the role of domestic flights in connecting distant cities.

Australia’s Top 10 Busiest Domestic Flight Routes

Passengers carried on the ten busiest domestic routes, July 2025–June
2026 (both directions combined).

Key findings

Melbourne–Sydney Leads Passenger Volume

Melbourne–Sydney carried 8.1 million passengers, nearly double Brisbane–Sydney (4.4 million), accounting for 15% of passenger traffic across the 62 routes.

Major East Coast Routes Carry High Traffic

The three routes connecting Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane carried 16.2 million passengers, representing 30% of total traffic across the 62 routes.

Monthly Seat Occupancy Across Australia’s Busiest Domestic Flight Routes

Monthly load factor (%) across the 15 busiest domestic
flight routes, July 2025–June 2026. Darker blue indicates a
higher proportion of seats occupied.

Key findings

Gold Coast routes show high occupancy

Melbourne–Gold Coast reached 93% occupancy in May and August, while Gold Coast–Sydney peaked at 94% in November, indicating strong seat utilisation on these routes.

Lower occupancy on selected routes

Canberra–Melbourne fell to 71%, while Brisbane–Townsville reached 70% in February. These rates indicate a larger proportion of unfilled seats, potentially reflecting differences in demand or airline capacity.

Monthly occupancy varies by route

Some routes maintain high occupancy throughout the year, while others experience monthly declines. This suggests that seat utilisation patterns differ across routes rather than following one consistent seasonal trend.

Section 3

The Rhythm of Domestic Air Travel: Seasonality and Demand

Australia’s Domestic Air Travel Calendar: Seasonal Passenger Patterns

Monthly domestic passenger activity from 2015 to 2025. Each
concentric ring represents a year, from 2015 at the
centre to 2025 on the outside; segments progress clockwise
from January. Blue indicates months above that year’s average,
while red indicates months below average.

Key findings

Spring and early summer see higher activity

Passenger activity is above the annual monthly average in several years from October to December, coinciding with spring school holidays and the start of the summer holiday season. Holiday travel may contribute to this increase.

February frequently sees lower activity

Passenger activity in February falls below the annual monthly average in several years, suggesting a recurring lull after the summer holiday period.

Seasonal patterns vary across years

Monthly passenger activity differs across years, indicating that seasonal trends are not fixed and may be affected by changing travel conditions and exceptional events.

Monthly Passenger Patterns Across Selected Domestic Flight Routes

Average monthly passenger volumes from 2023–2025, expressed as percentage deviations
from each route’s typical monthly level. Positive values indicate above-average
traffic; negative values indicate below-average traffic.

Key findings

Leisure Routes Show Distinct Seasonal Peaks

Cairns–Sydney peaks at 24% above its typical level in July, while Melbourne–Gold Coast peaks at 23% in January. The contrasting peaks indicate that seasonal passenger demand varies by destination.

Business Corridors Vary in Stability

Brisbane–Sydney and Melbourne–Sydney peak modestly in October (+11% and +9%), while Canberra–Sydney ranges from −33% in January to +18% in November. This shows that monthly fluctuations differ substantially even among business corridors.

Mining Routes Peak in October

Kalgoorlie–Perth and Perth–Port Hedland both reach their highest passenger levels in October. This pattern may reflect mining-related and FIFO work travel, although further data would be needed to confirm the cause.

Section 4

Beyond the Flight: Air Travel Purposes and Behaviour

Domestic Air Travel Growth Across Major Routes

Change in domestic trips by state visited, year ending June
2026 compared with the year before. Use the menu to
switch between all trips and overnight trips. * Smaller states
and territories have higher survey error.

Pinch the trackpad (or Ctrl + scroll) to zoom · drag or two-finger swipe to move

Key findings

All States and Territories Recorded Growth

Domestic trips increased across all states and territories, ranging from 2.3% in Tasmania to 7.7% in Queensland. This suggests that growth was widespread, although its magnitude varied by destination.

Growth Was Stronger in Some States

Queensland and Western Australia grew most, possibly reflecting coastal and nature-based tourism in Queensland, and tourism and mining travel in Western Australia. Tasmania, the NT and the ACT grew more modestly, perhaps due to differences in demand and market size. These are possible explanations, not confirmed causes.

Why Australians Fly: Business and Leisure Travel

Share of domestic trips by main purpose for the year
ending June 2026. Each square represents 1% of trips, comparing
all trips with overnight trips.

Key findings

Holidays Drive Domestic Air Travel

Holidays account for the largest share of domestic trips, highlighting the importance of leisure travel and Australia’s natural attractions in shaping domestic air travel demand.

Family Visits Sustain Domestic Mobility

Visiting friends and relatives is another major travel purpose, reflecting the role of family connections and social gatherings in domestic travel.

Business Travel Serves a Different Purpose

Business accounts for a smaller share of domestic trips than holidays and family visits, reflecting its more specialised role in travel between commercial centres.

Diverging Trends in Domestic Air Travel by Purpose, 2025–2026

Percentage change in trips in each quarter compared with the
June quarter 2025 (0% = no change).

Key findings

Business Travel Declines Throughout the Year

Business trips fell in every quarter, reaching 18% below the June 2025 baseline by June 2026. This contrasts with the growth in leisure-related travel, suggesting differing travel patterns by purpose.

Holiday Travel Records the Strongest Growth

Holiday trips rose steadily to 9% above the baseline by June 2026, highlighting growing leisure travel. Australia’s beaches, national parks and coastal landscapes offer opportunities for sightseeing, outdoor recreation and nature-based holidays.

Conclusion

Australia’s domestic aviation remains essential

Domestic air travel connects major cities and regional destinations across
Australia. Despite the pandemic disruption, passenger demand has largely recovered,
although recovery varies across routes and seasonal travel patterns.
